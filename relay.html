What makes RELAY different

Three decisions
most tools don’t make.

01
No grade, no judgement

Purely formative. A student can admit what they don’t understand without it costing them a mark. That honesty is the whole mechanism: it is what makes confusion visible in the first place.

02
It informs, it never decides

RELAY reports what it saw and stops. It does not recommend an action, contact a student, or put anything on a report. Your teachers keep the professional judgement that only they can exercise.

03
Built around your school, not off the shelf

RELAY is configured to your curriculum, your ethos and your cohort, and draws only on the material your teachers supply. The guardrails are real, and they are yours to set. This is not a subscription you switch on and hope suits you.

For students

What a student
gets out of it.

RELAY is usually described by what a teacher sees. This is the other half: what changes for the student sitting in the room.

Somewhere to say “I don’t understand”

Around half the students we surveyed said they are uncomfortable telling a teacher they haven’t understood something. So they say nothing, and the lesson moves on. RELAY gives them a way to say it that isn’t in front of the class and doesn’t cost them a mark.

Support that steps back

RELAY is built on co-regulation: more structure early, less of it over time. The aim is a student who can judge their own understanding without an adult having to ask first.

Confidence to attempt more

A student who is sure they have understood something is readier to take a risk on a harder question, or a subject they had written off. This is the outcome we care most about.

For teachers

A formative assessment partner
for learning environments.

RELAY works alongside the assessment a school already does. It carries no marks and reaches no conclusions. It gives a teacher a clearer view of where a group actually is, in the classroom and beyond it.

Visibility before it counts

Where a class has actually landed on a concept, in the students’ own words, well before a summative assessment is sitting in front of them. Early enough that there is still something a teacher can do about it.

Conversations you can prepare for

Rather than approaching a student cold, a teacher walks in already knowing what the difficulty is. The same holds for a conversation with a colleague, a head of faculty, or a parent.

No extra marking

Nothing here is graded, so nothing here is added to a marking pile. The point is to lower what a teacher has to hold in their head, not to hand them another system to keep on top of.

The research underneath

Every design decision
traces to a finding.

RELAY is built from published learning science rather than from assumptions about what AI should do in a classroom. Each choice below links to the work it came from.

No grade, no judgement Black & Wiliam, 1998

Formative assessment produces some of the largest measured gains in education, but only where feedback is separated from grading. Attach a mark and students attend to the mark rather than the feedback. Removing the grade is what makes the feedback do any work.

Black, P. & Wiliam, D. (1998). Assessment and Classroom Learning. doi.org/10.1080/0969595980050102

Support that steps back over time Zimmerman, 2002 · Torrington et al., 2024

Self-regulated learning is learnable rather than innate. Students move towards it through support they gradually need less of. RELAY is built to work that way: more guidance early, less as a student shows they can carry the reasoning themselves.

Zimmerman, B. (2002). Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner. doi.org/10.1207/s15430421tip4102_2

The teacher stays in the loop McCaslin & Hickey, 2001

Co-regulation describes the adult support that precedes self-regulation. It is a relationship, not a feature. This is why RELAY routes a student’s confusion to their teacher rather than resolving it alone: the point is to prompt the conversation, not to replace it.

McCaslin, M. & Hickey, D. (2001). Self-regulated learning and academic achievement: A Vygotskian view. In B. Zimmerman & D. Schunk (Eds.), Self-Regulated Learning and Academic Achievement (2nd ed.). doi.org/10.4324/9781410601032

Why the relationship is the target Van Bergen et al., 2023 · Burns et al., 2022

Student-teacher relationship quality predicts engagement, behaviour and achievement. It is also uneven: a substantial minority of students describe their relationship with their teacher as difficult or distant. Making a student’s thinking visible gives a teacher something concrete to respond to.

Burns, E. et al. (2022), Journal of Adolescence. doi.org/10.1002/jad.12093

Guardrails change the outcome Bastani et al., PNAS 2025

In a randomised trial with nearly a thousand secondary students, a carefully designed, guardrailed tool produced a substantial lift in performance. Unrestricted access did not. How a tool is designed to behave with a student decides whether it helps.

Bastani, H. et al. (2025), PNAS. doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
